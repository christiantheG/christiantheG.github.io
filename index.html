Research direction

Communication built around decisions.

My work connects physical-layer representation learning with resource allocation and downstream inference. The central question is simple: how can a system spend its limited communication budget on the information that changes the decision?

01

Semantic communication

Learning compact representations that preserve task value instead of reconstructing every bit or pixel.

02

Adaptive wireless systems

Adapting to channel quality, symbol budgets, and Age of Information without costly online retraining.

03

Learning for allocation

Combining reinforcement learning, optimization, and information-theoretic structure to schedule scarce resources.

Selected scholarship

Research in context.

Recent work spans cross-layer semantic communication, real-time pedestrian-safety monitoring, and multi-agent reinforcement learning for edge systems.

ACM MobiHoc ’26 Submitted Co-first author

Significance-Driven Semantic Communication

Christian McDowell∗, Andrea Panebianco∗, Sirin Chakraborty, and Yin Sun

Defines per-sample data significance through L-divergence and unifies a Meta-VIB transceiver with Q-Maximization scheduling. The system adapts across channel quality, codelength, and information age without online retraining; on a real-world pedestrian-safety dataset, it reaches up to 1000× semantic-spectrum-efficiency gain at 0 dB average SNR and 40× at 5 dB.

ACM MobiHoc ’26 Workshop In preparation Co-first author

SafeStep: An Interactive Demonstration of Semantic Communication for Pedestrian Safety Monitoring

Christian McDowell∗, Andrea Panebianco∗, Jeremiah Yang∗, Sirin Chakraborty, Samuel Chamoun, Travis Ross, and Yin Sun

Presents an interactive, live semantic-communication system for pedestrian-safety monitoring at Toomer’s Corner. Visitors can compare methods while varying SNR, codelength, and Age of Information to see how a compact model prioritizes safety-critical information under changing channel conditions.
